Texas has recently established specific legal frameworks to address "dark patterns" and manipulative artificial intelligence (AI) practices through two primary laws: the Texas Data Privacy and Security Act (TDPSA) and the Texas Responsible Artificial Intelligence Governance Act (TRAIGA). These laws define dark patterns as manipulative design choices that subvert user autonomy. [1, 2, 3] 
## Legal Definitions in Texas

* Dark Pattern Definition: Texas law defines a dark pattern as a "user interface designed or manipulated with the effect of substantially subverting or impairing user autonomy, decision-making, or choice".
* Scope of "Consent": Under the Texas Data Privacy and Security Act (TDPSA), any agreement or consent obtained through the use of dark patterns is legally invalid.
* Federal Alignment: Texas statutes explicitly include any practice that the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) designates as a dark pattern within their own definition. [4, 5, 6, 7, 8] 

## Texas Responsible Artificial Intelligence Governance Act (TRAIGA) [9] 
Signed into law in June 2025 and effective January 1, 2026, TRAIGA introduces specific prohibitions against manipulative AI: [1, 9, 10, 11] 

* Behavioral Manipulation: The act categorically prohibits developing or deploying AI systems "intentionally aimed to incite or encourage harm to another person, physical self-harm, or crime".
* Mandatory Disclosure: Government agencies and certain private sectors (like healthcare) must disclose when a user is interacting with an AI. These disclosures must be "clear, conspicuous, written in plain English, and free of dark patterns".
* Enforcement: There is no private right of action; only the Texas Attorney General has the authority to enforce these rules. [9, 11, 12, 13, 14] 

## Texas Deceptive Trade Practices Act (DTPA)
While TRAIGA is new, the Texas Attorney General often uses the Texas Deceptive Trade Practices-Consumer Protection Act (DTPA) to investigate AI developers. [14] 

* Investigations: Attorney General Ken Paxton has issued Civil Investigative Demands (CIDs) to AI companies for allegedly engaging in deceptive practices, such as marketing AI as "confidential counseling" while exploiting user data or impersonating licensed professionals.
* Penalties: Violations can result in significant civil penalties, including fines up to $200,000 per violation for uncurable offenses under the new TRAIGA framework. [14, 15, 16, 17] 

## Recognized "Manipulative Patterns" [3] 
In practice, Texas regulators look for the following "dark patterns" often used to control or mislead users: [3, 18, 19] 

* Choice Architecture: Design that steers users into specific choices that benefit the company rather than the user.
* Linguistic Manipulation: Using confusing language, double negatives, or "therapy-speak" to obscure a user's rights or the system's actual functions.
* Obscured Disclosures: Hiding important terms in lengthy documents or behind multiple clicks to prevent informed consent. [3, 5, 20, 21]
